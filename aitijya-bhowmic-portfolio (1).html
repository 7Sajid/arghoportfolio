I completed my B.Sc. in Electrical and Electronic Engineering at Chittagong University of Engineering & Technology (CUET), Bangladesh, where I built my foundation in power systems, embedded design, and IoT. 03 — current research

From signal to superposition

At the University of South Carolina, I work as a Graduate Research Assistant in Prof. Alphan Sahin's lab, contributing to research that sits at the intersection of wireless communication theory and quantum information.

01

Over-the-Air Computation & Foundations

Exploring reliable, non-coherent waveforms and modulation schemes that let a network compute functions directly over the wireless channel — instead of communicating, then computing.

02

Quantum Information Theory

Studying quantum error correction and QKD-secured communication for resilient power-grid coordination, as part of a project funded by SC Quantum — aimed at hardening grid cybersecurity against quantum-era threats.

03

Lab context

The group's foundational OAC research is supported in part by an NSF CAREER Award (2025–2030), advancing reliable, scalable over-the-air computation for wireless networks.

04 — b.sc. thesis

IoT-based home energy management to cut peak-hour costs

Supervisor — Prof. Dr. Nur Mohammad, PhD, Dept. of EEE, CUET

For my final-year project, I designed and built a home load-management system that gives residential users real-time visibility and remote control over their appliances. The system reads live current draw, warns users as consumption approaches a peak-hour threshold, and — when demand is highest — can shift the load automatically from the grid to a battery-integrated renewable source. It won a place at the 10th IEEE International Conference on Power Systems (ICPS 2023) in Cox's Bazar.
